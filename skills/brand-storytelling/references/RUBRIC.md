# Rubric (0–2)

Score the Brand Storytelling Pack. A “ship-ready” pack typically scores **≥ 9/12** with no dimension at 0.

## 1) Audience + objective fit (0–2)
- **0:** Primary audience is not identified. No channel specified. No CTA or desired action after hearing the story.
- **1:** Audience, channel, and CTA are named, but language and structure are not tailored to the audience’s context (e.g., investor story reads like a customer pitch).
- **2:** Audience, channel, and CTA are crisp. Language, examples, and emotional hooks are tailored to the primary audience’s motivations. Script structure matches channel constraints (deck vs. social vs. website).

## 2) Five-second moment + narrative arc (0–2)
- **0:** No identifiable turning point. Story is a generic company timeline (“we founded, we built, we launched”) with no emotional anchor.
- **1:** A turning point exists and can be stated in 1-2 sentences, but the narrative arc (context -> tension -> moment -> new belief) is incomplete or the moment feels interchangeable with other companies in the category.
- **2:** Singular five-second moment is vivid, specific to this company, and could not be copy-pasted onto a competitor. The arc builds context and tension before the moment, then lands a new belief with clear stakes. A reader remembers the moment after one pass.

## 3) Brand perception coherence (0–2)
- **0:** No brand perception target (purpose/positioning/personality) is stated. Story feels disconnected from brand identity.
- **1:** Perception target is stated but the story contradicts or ignores parts of it (e.g., target says “scrappy” but story sounds corporate). At least one element (purpose, positioning, or personality) is inconsistent.
- **2:** Purpose, positioning, and personality reinforce each other. Every story beat, word choice, and example supports the target perception. A reader who only saw the story would correctly infer the brand’s desired identity.

## 4) Proof + credibility (0–2)
- **0:** Key claims (traction, impact, differentiation) have no proof points. Story feels like aspiration with no evidence.
- **1:** Some claims have proof (metrics, customer quotes, concrete examples), but at least one major claim is unsupported and not flagged as a placeholder.
- **2:** Every key claim in the narrative is backed by a specific proof point (metric, quote, example) or is explicitly labeled “to validate” with a next step. Proof bank maps claims to evidence and flags gaps.

## 5) Script usability across channels (0–2)
- **0:** Only one script length exists, or scripts are not matched to any channel (too long for social, too short for a talk, etc.). Scripts sound like marketing copy, not natural speech.
- **1:** Multiple lengths exist (e.g., 2-min and 30-sec) but one or more scripts need significant editing before use. Website paragraph exists but reads like a transcript, not web copy.
- **2:** 2-minute, 30-second, and website paragraph versions are all immediately usable. Each is tailored to its channel (talk track is sayable aloud, website copy scans visually, social version hooks in the first line). CTA is embedded in each version.

## 6) Distribution readiness + Q&A prep (0–2)
- **0:** No plan for how or where the story will be used. No Q&A preparation for follow-up questions.
- **1:** Build-in-public or distribution plan exists with content pillars and a cadence, but lacks named owners or is unrealistic for stated capacity. Q&A bank covers some questions but answers are paragraph-length instead of pithy.
- **2:** Distribution plan has content pillars, a weekly cadence, named owners, and guardrails (what not to share). Q&A bank covers the top 5-10 likely questions with one-sentence pithy answers backed by evidence. Plan is feasible for stated team capacity.
