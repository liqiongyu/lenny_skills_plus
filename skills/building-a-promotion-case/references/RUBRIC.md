# Rubric (Score 0/1/2)

Score the Promotion Case Pack. Target: **≥ 10/14**, with no category at **0**.

0 = missing or unusable | 1 = present but weak | 2 = clear, specific, and reusable

## 1) Target clarity + timing (0–2)
- **0:** No explicit target level or promo window; the reader cannot tell what promotion is being sought or when.
- **1:** Target level and timing are stated, but the “why now” is missing or the promo process/committee mechanics are vague.
- **2:** Crisp target level, specific promo cycle/date, clear “why now” tied to business context, and calendar/process constraints (committee dates, submission deadlines) are explicit.

## 2) Ladder/expectations alignment (0–2)
- **0:** No ladder mapping; claims are generic (“I do great work”) with no link to competency definitions.
- **1:** Some competencies are referenced, but observable behaviors are only partially specified; gaps are acknowledged without a concrete plan to close them.
- **2:** Clear competency map with specific next-level signals (observable behaviors, not adjectives), explicit gap list, and a time-bound plan to close each gap before the promo window.

## 3) Evidence strength (0–2)
- **0:** Evidence is mostly opinions and activities (“attended meetings,” “worked hard”); fewer than 2 claims have metrics or artifacts.
- **1:** Some outcomes and artifacts exist, but evidence is uneven — at least one major claim lacks a metric/proxy or stakeholder corroboration.
- **2:** Strong evidence log where every major claim has at least 2 evidence types (metric/proxy + artifact or stakeholder proof); evidence is linked to specific ladder competencies.

## 4) Narrative + skimmability (0–2)
- **0:** Rambling doc with no clear structure; a reader cannot find the thesis or key claims within 3 minutes.
- **1:** Reasonably structured with sections, but the thesis is buried or some claims lack clear evidence pointers; requires more than one read-through.
- **2:** Executive-friendly one-pager with a bold thesis up front; every claim has a pointer to evidence; a decision-maker can skim and form an opinion in under 3 minutes.

## 5) Next-level scope + leverage (0–2)
- **0:** Work described is scoped at the current level; no evidence of cross-team influence, ambiguity navigation, or leverage.
- **1:** Some next-level behaviors are present but inconsistent; leverage examples are isolated rather than sustained.
- **2:** Sustained next-level behaviors are demonstrated (navigating ambiguity, cross-team influence, multiplier/leverage effects, high-quality decisions under uncertainty) with concrete examples.

## 6) Manager alignment plan (0–2)
- **0:** Manager is not involved in the plan; no agreed criteria or check-ins exist.
- **1:** Some alignment exists (e.g., manager is aware), but success criteria are not mutually agreed, or check-in schedule is missing.
- **2:** Clear “Magic Loop” plan with explicitly agreed criteria, documented manager commitments, scheduled check-ins (dates), and a script for the alignment conversation.

## 7) Sponsor/visibility plan + risk handling (0–2)
- **0:** No sponsor plan; ignores organizational politics and risks entirely.
- **1:** Basic stakeholder list exists with some outreach ideas, but asks are vague (“get visibility”) and risks/open questions are generic or missing.
- **2:** Sponsor map names specific people with specific asks and timelines; no-surprises plan covers key decision-makers; risks, open questions, and next steps are explicit with owners and dates.
