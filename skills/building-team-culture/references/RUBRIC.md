# Rubric (Score 1–5)

Score the Team Culture Operating System Pack. Target: **≥ 24/35**, with no category below **3**.

## 1) Clarity of scope + target state (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No target state or scope defined; the pack is just a list of aspirations.
- **1:** Vague culture aspirations; no clear “more true in 8 weeks” target; scope is unbounded.
- **2:** Some target behaviors mentioned but they are not observable or time-bound; scope is implicit rather than stated.
- **3:** Target behaviors are mostly clear; scope boundaries exist; but “more true in 8 weeks” is not specific enough to measure.
- **4:** Crisp target state with observable behaviors; scope is bounded; minor gap in timeline specificity or one target lacks a leading indicator.
- **5:** Crisp target state with observable behaviors and explicit boundaries. A team member could tell you in 8 weeks whether each target is “more true.”

## 2) Evidence-based diagnosis (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No diagnosis or culture snapshot.
- **1:** Generic diagnosis; no examples (e.g., “communication is poor” without specifics).
- **2:** A few examples exist but root causes are not explored; stated vs lived culture distinction is absent.
- **3:** Some examples; plausible root causes; but sacred cows are not identified or stated/lived gap is not analyzed.
- **4:** Good examples with root causes; stated vs lived culture is distinguished; most sacred cows are identified but one or two sensitive areas are avoided.
- **5:** Strong examples; separates stated vs lived culture; sacred cows are identified and explicitly named with a recommendation to challenge or accept each.

## 3) Culture code quality (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No culture code or principles.
- **1:** Generic values with no behaviors (e.g., “We value innovation” with no definition of what that means day-to-day).
- **2:** Principles exist with some behavioral examples but lack decision rules or anti-patterns; a team could not use them to resolve a disagreement.
- **3:** Principles include some behaviors and basic do/don’t; but anti-patterns or decision rules are missing for most principles.
- **4:** Principles are behavior-based with do/don’t and decision rules; anti-patterns exist for most but measurable signals are missing for 1-2 principles.
- **5:** Principles are behavior-based with decision rules, anti-patterns, and measurable signals. Two team members reading the code would resolve the same hypothetical conflict the same way.

## 4) Operationalization (norms + rituals) (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No norms or rituals defined.
- **1:** No norms/rituals or they’re ceremonial (e.g., “be respectful in meetings” without specifying what that means).
- **2:** Some norms exist but are not tied to specific culture principles; rituals lack owners or agendas.
- **3:** Some norms and rituals; ownership partially defined; but the mapping between norms/rituals and culture principles is incomplete.
- **4:** Norms cover the key areas (communication, decisions, feedback, conflict); rituals have owners and agendas; minor gap in one area.
- **5:** Norms reduce ambiguity in the team’s most common friction points; rituals have owners, agendas, outputs, and explicitly map to culture principles.

## 5) Reinforcement plan realism (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No reinforcement or rollout plan.
- **1:** “Announce the doc” plan; no ownership; no channels beyond initial communication.
- **2:** Some rollout steps exist but are one-time actions (e.g., “share in all-hands”) with no ongoing reinforcement.
- **3:** Basic rollout steps; some reinforcement channels (e.g., hiring, onboarding mentions); but DRIs and timelines are unclear.
- **4:** Concrete rollout with 30/60/90 view; coaching and onboarding hooks exist; one or two DRIs are not explicitly assigned.
- **5:** Concrete 30/60/90 plan with coaching model, onboarding/hiring hooks, promotion criteria alignment, and assigned DRIs for each reinforcement channel.

## 6) Psychological safety + conflict handling (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No mention of psychological safety or conflict resolution.
- **1:** Safety is mentioned but not operationalized; conflict norms are absent.
- **2:** Safety is acknowledged as important but practices are generic (“create a safe space”); conflict is mentioned but no resolution mechanism exists.
- **3:** Includes some safety practices (e.g., blameless retros, anonymous feedback); conflict norms exist but lack specificity on escalation or repair.
- **4:** Safety practices are concrete with specific leader behaviors; conflict norms include escalation paths; minor gap in repair loops or post-conflict follow-up.
- **5:** Safety practices are concrete; leaders’ specific behaviors are specified (e.g., “leader speaks last”, “acknowledge mistakes publicly”); repair loops exist with clear steps for after trust is broken.

## 7) Measurement + iteration (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No measurement or iteration plan.
- **1:** No measurement; no iteration plan; culture code is treated as permanent.
- **2:** Vague mention of “checking in” but no specific metrics, pulse questions, or cadence.
- **3:** Some metrics or pulse questions exist; basic cadence (e.g., quarterly review); but metrics are not tied to specific focus areas.
- **4:** Leading indicators and pulse questions exist tied to focus areas; iteration cadence is defined; minor gap in who owns the review or how findings lead to changes.
- **5:** Leading indicators + pulse questions + iteration cadence tied to rituals and focus areas. Clear mechanism for updating the culture code based on what’s learned.
