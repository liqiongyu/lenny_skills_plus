# Rubric (0–2)

Score the Community Building Pack. A “ship-ready” pack typically scores **≥ 9/12** with no dimension at 0.

## 1) Goal + audience fit (0–2)
- **0:** Primary goal is missing or generic (“build community”). No timebox. No success metric. Target member profile is vague or identical to the product ICP with no community-specific criteria.
- **1:** Primary goal is stated with a timebox and at least one metric, but the metric is a vanity number (e.g., “1,000 members”) rather than a behavioral signal. Member criteria exist but lack specificity on role, seniority, or motivation.
- **2:** Primary goal is crisp, timeboxed, and measured by a behavioral metric (e.g., “50% of seed cohort completes first-win action within 2 weeks”). Member criteria specify role, seniority/experience, motivation to join, and where they currently gather.

## 2) Movement + value exchange strength (0–2)
- **0:** No community thesis. No articulated reason to join beyond “networking” or “get updates.” Value exchange is absent or one-directional (company broadcasts, members consume).
- **1:** Community thesis exists with a stated worldview or “enemy,” but it could apply to most companies in the category. Value exchange names what members get and contribute, but the “get” is generic (e.g., “learn from peers”) without specifics.
- **2:** Thesis is non-generic and could not be copy-pasted onto a competitor. It names a specific worldview, what the community stands for, and what it stands against. Value exchange includes concrete benefits (early access, expert AMAs, job board, templates) and concrete contributions (answers, content, feedback, referrals).

## 3) Platform strategy realism (0–2)
- **0:** Platform is chosen by trend or founder preference with no evidence that target members use it. No outpost plan. No onboarding flow.
- **1:** Home base is chosen with some rationale (“our users are on Slack”). Outposts are listed, but some lack owners or a value-first contribution plan. Onboarding exists but the “empty room problem” is not addressed.
- **2:** Home base is chosen based on evidence of where members already gather. Each outpost has a named owner, a value-first contribution plan, and a route-back mechanism. Onboarding flow solves the empty room problem (pre-seeded content, welcome messages, starter threads). Influencer/social-graph shortlist identifies specific connectors.

## 4) Launch + programming executability (0–2)
- **0:** No seeding plan. No programming calendar. No outreach scripts. Launch is a vague “open the doors” without structure.
- **1:** 30/60/90 plan exists with a seed cohort strategy and a 4+ week programming calendar, but some rituals lack facilitators or goals. Outreach scripts exist but are generic.
- **2:** Seeding plan names a specific seed cohort (size, criteria, invite source) with personalized outreach scripts. Programming calendar covers 4-8 weeks with specific rituals (AMAs, office hours, challenges), each with a named facilitator, a goal, and at least one member-generated slot per week. Templates exist for key rituals.

## 5) Governance + safety (0–2)
- **0:** No community rules. No moderation plan. No escalation path. No privacy guidelines.
- **1:** Community rules exist and cover basic conduct. Moderation roles are identified, but escalation paths are vague (e.g., “contact admin”) and privacy guidelines are incomplete.
- **2:** Community rules cover conduct, content standards, and privacy norms. Moderation workflow has named moderators with capacity matched to platform volume. Escalation ladder is explicit (warning -> mute -> ban) with documented criteria. Privacy guidelines address data handling, screenshots, and member consent. Offboarding process exists for the ambassador program.

## 6) Measurement + operating cadence (0–2)
- **0:** No metrics defined. No review loop. No connection between community data and product/business decisions.
- **1:** Metrics exist (member count, active members, posts) but are not mapped to journey stages. Weekly ops cadence is mentioned but lacks specific decision rules or a product feedback loop.
- **2:** Metrics are mapped to journey stages (discover -> join -> first win -> habit -> advocate) with a target for each stage. Weekly ops cadence has specific review items, decision rules (e.g., “if first-win rate drops below X%, run an onboarding experiment”), and a product/feedback loop that routes community insights to the product team.
