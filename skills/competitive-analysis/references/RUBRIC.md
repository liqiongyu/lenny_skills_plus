# Rubric (1–5)

Score the Competitive Analysis Pack. A “ship-ready” pack is typically **≥ 22/30** with no category below 3.

## 1) Decision clarity (1–5)
- **1:** No decision stated; looks like general research notes.
- **2:** A decision is implied but not explicitly named; audience is unclear.
- **3:** Decision and audience are stated; scope is mostly clear.
- **4:** Decision, audience, and scope are clear; deadline or success criteria partially defined.
- **5:** Decision, deadline, and success criteria are explicit; pack is obviously decision-useful.

## 2) Alternatives coverage (1–5)
- **1:** Only direct competitors listed.
- **2:** Direct and indirect competitors listed but status quo, workarounds, and non-consumption are missing.
- **3:** Includes status quo and at least one non-obvious alternative.
- **4:** Comprehensive map with status quo, workarounds, and indirect alternatives; “true deal alternative” is identified for most segments.
- **5:** Clear competitive alternatives map that identifies the true deal alternative(s) per segment with notes on switching cost and adoption friction.

## 3) Evidence quality (1–5)
- **1:** Unsupported claims; no sources; certainty without proof.
- **2:** A few evidence points exist but most claims are opinion; no confidence labels.
- **3:** Some evidence; confidence labeling is inconsistent.
- **4:** Most claims have evidence (links, quotes, data); confidence is labeled but a few gaps remain.
- **5:** Evidence-backed claims with confidence/unknowns explicit and no reliance on confidential info.

## 4) Customer POV + criteria (1–5)
- **1:** Feature checklist; little about customer outcomes.
- **2:** Criteria mix features and outcomes; customer language is absent; comparison is vendor-centric.
- **3:** Criteria reflect customer needs but miss key decision drivers.
- **4:** Criteria are customer-outcome-framed; matrix covers most alternatives but one or two decision drivers are under-explored.
- **5:** Criteria + matrix clearly explain why each alternative wins/loses for the customer.

## 5) Differentiation + positioning (1–5)
- **1:** Generic value prop that could apply to anyone.
- **2:** Some differentiation stated but not tied to a specific competitor or alternative; no proof points.
- **3:** Some differentiation, but not anchored to a specific alternative.
- **4:** 1-2 positioning hypotheses name specific alternatives with proof points; tradeoffs are partially stated.
- **5:** 2–3 crisp positioning hypotheses explicitly “against X” with proof points and tradeoffs.

## 6) Actionability (1–5)
- **1:** No clear next actions; only observations.
- **2:** A few recommendations listed but no battlecards, no monitoring plan, and weak linkage to analysis.
- **3:** Some recommendations, but weak linkage to findings.
- **4:** Recommendations tie to findings; battlecards exist for top competitors; monitoring plan is present but cadence or triggers are vague.
- **5:** Specific recommendations + battlecards + monitoring plan that can be executed immediately.
