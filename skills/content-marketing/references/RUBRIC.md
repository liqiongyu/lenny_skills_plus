# Rubric (Content Marketing Plan Pack)

Score each dimension 0–2. A “ship-ready” pack typically scores **≥ 9/12** with no dimension at 0.

## 1) ICP + content market fit clarity
- **0:** ICP is missing or a single generic sentence (e.g., “marketers”). No audience anxieties or content promises listed.
- **1:** ICP names a role and company stage. Anxieties and promises exist but are generic (e.g., “wants to grow”) or not mapped to specific content types.
- **2:** ICP includes role, seniority, company size/stage, and buying context. At least 3 anxieties are mapped to specific, repeatable content promises with named content types (how-to, teardown, template, etc.).

## 2) Demand validation rigor (SEO)
- **0:** Topics are chosen from brainstorms or intuition with zero demand evidence. No confidence labels.
- **1:** Some topics have demand signals (autocomplete, competitor pages, keyword tool data) but coverage is inconsistent. “To validate” items exist without concrete next steps.
- **2:** Every SEO topic cites a specific demand signal (search volume range, Search Console data, competitor ranking page, or autocomplete evidence). Non-SEO topics have a named distribution owner and channel. All “to validate” items have a concrete next step and owner.

## 3) Differentiation / information gain
- **0:** Content briefs and topics could be written by any company in the category. No unique POV, proprietary data, or original examples.
- **1:** Some pieces reference a unique angle or SME insight, but at least half of the briefs rely on generic advice or restate commonly available information.
- **2:** Every content brief specifies what must be uniquely true (proprietary data, SME experience, original framework, or customer story). The “information gain” over existing top-ranking content is explicit in each brief.

## 4) Channel focus + executability
- **0:** Three or more primary channels with no clear priority. Calendar has no owners, or requires more capacity than available.
- **1:** One primary channel is named. Calendar exists for 4+ weeks with publish dates, but some items lack owners or the review workflow has gaps.
- **2:** Single primary channel for 4-8 weeks with a named owner per piece, explicit review checkpoints, and a repurposing plan that does not create unassigned work. Calendar math checks out against stated team capacity.

## 5) AI safety + quality controls
- **0:** AI is used for drafting with no human review gates. No guidance on allowed/disallowed AI uses. No fact-checking step.
- **1:** AI SOP exists with some human review steps, but the boundary between AI-generated and human-added content is unclear. Fact/claim checking is mentioned but not built into the workflow.
- **2:** AI SOP defines specific allowed uses (outline, research, first draft) and disallowed uses (final claims, quotes, data). Every piece has a mandatory human review gate. Fact/claim check is a named workflow step with an owner.

## 6) Measurement + iteration loop
- **0:** No metrics defined. No review cadence. No decision rules for what to do with data.
- **1:** Primary metric and 1-2 leading indicators are listed. Review cadence is mentioned but lacks decision rules (e.g., what happens if a piece underperforms).
- **2:** Primary metric + 2-3 leading indicators with baselines or targets. Weekly review cadence with explicit decision rules: when to double down, when to cut a topic, when to iterate on distribution. Dashboard or tracking method is specified.
