# Rubric (Score the Pack)

Score each category **0–2** and sum. Use the notes to decide what to fix.

Scale:
- **0 = Missing / unacceptable**
- **1 = Partial / unclear**
- **2 = Strong / clear / decision-ready**

## Scoring table

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem anchor clarity | No target customer or problem stated; vision floats without grounding. | Customer and problem are mentioned but generic (e.g., "businesses need better tools") or could apply to many products. | Specific customer segment + potent user problem stated in 1-2 sentences; a reader can immediately say "yes, that's real and important." |
| Future state concreteness (not vague) | Vision is a tagline or slogan with no concrete description of the future. | Future state is described but in abstract terms ("seamless experience"); hard to picture what changes. | Future state describes who benefits, how their life is different, and what the product looks like in 5-10 years; passes the "could a competitor say this?" test (no). |
| Lofty + realistic balance | Either pure fantasy (colonize Mars-level) or incremental feature list with no ambition. | Aspirational but missing a plausible path, or realistic but uninspiring. | Ambitious enough to motivate multi-year investment AND grounded enough that a skeptical exec can see a credible path from today. |
| Tech-agnostic framing | Vision is defined by a specific technology ("we'll use AI/blockchain to..."). | Mostly outcome-focused but slips into implementation language in places. | Entirely framed around user outcomes and value; technology is mentioned only as an enabler, never as the vision itself. |
| Decision usefulness (tie-breaker) | Vision does not help resolve any real product debate; purely decorative. | Vision provides general direction but is too broad to resolve a specific tradeoff. | A product team can point to the vision to settle a real prioritization or scope disagreement; it functions as a tie-breaker. |
| Pillars imply choices | Pillars are generic values (e.g., "quality", "innovation") that any company would claim. | Pillars are relevant but at least one lacks a clear "we will NOT do X" consequence. | Each pillar forces a resource allocation decision and implies at least one explicit non-goal or tradeoff. |
| Strategy bridge (choices + non-goals) | No strategic choices or non-goals listed; vision is disconnected from action. | Some choices listed but fewer than 3 non-goals, or non-goals are trivial/obvious. | 3-5 explicit choices + at least 3 meaningful non-goals that stakeholders would debate; choices are clearly tied to pillars. |
| Wedge/form factor plausibility | No near-term wedge or form factor proposed; gap between vision and today is unaddressed. | A wedge is mentioned but its connection to the long-term vision is hand-wavy. | A specific, plausible near-term wedge is proposed that delivers immediate value AND visibly progresses the 5-10 year vision; path from wedge to full vision is sketched. |
| Rollout/alignment readiness | No alignment plan; pack is a document with no plan for adoption. | Alignment plan exists but is generic ("share with leadership"); no cadence or stakeholder-specific actions. | Named stakeholders, specific review format (e.g., workshop, async review), cadence for revisiting, and anticipated objections with pre-built responses. |
| Risks/open questions/next steps quality | Section missing or contains only boilerplate ("more research needed"). | Risks and questions are listed but are generic; next steps lack owners or deadlines. | 3+ specific risks with mitigation ideas, open questions tied to named owners, and next steps with clear actions and timelines. |

## Interpretation
- **18–20:** Strong. Ready to share and align.
- **14–17:** Usable but has gaps; fix the lowest-scoring categories.
- **<14:** Not decision-ready; revisit problem anchor, concreteness, and strategic choices/non-goals.
