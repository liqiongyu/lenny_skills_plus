# Showcase: Finding Mentors Sponsors

> Demonstrates the value of the `finding-mentors-sponsors` skill pack by comparing
> skill-guided output vs. a vanilla LLM response to the same prompt.

## Test Prompt

> Use `finding-mentors-sponsors`. I'm a mid-level PM (3 years) at a 500-person enterprise SaaS company. I want to grow into a leadership role but lack visibility outside my team. I need a mentor for strategic thinking and stakeholder management, and a sponsor who can advocate for me on high-visibility projects. I have about 2 hours/week, I'm comfortable with outreach but moved to this company 8 months ago so my internal network is thin. My manager is supportive but not well-connected to the exec team. Build a Mentor & Sponsor Plan Pack with a target list of 15 candidates (internal directors + external product leaders), warm-path map, 3 outreach message templates, a first-meeting agenda, and a relationship tracking system. Output: Mentor & Sponsor Plan Pack.

## Key Differences

1. **Mentor vs. sponsor differentiation throughout.** The skill output maintains a clear separation between mentor tactics (advice-seeking outreach, learning-focused meetings) and sponsor tactics (earn-signal-ask sequence, evidence-based advocacy requests). The baseline explains the distinction upfront but the operational sections largely treat both relationship types the same way.

2. **Sponsor strategy with earn-signal-ask phases.** The skill output includes a structured 3-phase sponsor strategy: Phase 1 (Weeks 1-8) focuses on delivering a "bet-worthy outcome" with 4 specific options, Phase 2 (Weeks 4-12) builds visibility through artifacts that travel, and Phase 3 (Weeks 8-16+) makes concrete sponsor asks with a template. The baseline suggests earning sponsorship through general visibility work but does not provide a phased playbook.

3. **Warm-path specificity.** The skill output maps a specific warm path for each of 15 candidates (e.g., "ask your engineering lead for an intro" or "engage with 2-3 of their posts with substantive comments, then DM"). The baseline lists candidates by category with general advice on how to find them but does not provide per-candidate warm-path strategies.

4. **First-meeting question bank by topic.** The skill output provides a curated question bank organized by mentor type (strategic thinking, stakeholder management, org navigation, career path), with specific questions like "How do you pre-wire a decision with a senior stakeholder who might push back?" The baseline provides a meeting agenda with time blocks and general guidance but fewer ready-to-use questions.

5. **Relationship operating system with wind-down rules.** The skill output defines cadence, value exchange, and escalation/wind-down rules per relationship type (internal mentor, sponsor, external mentor, peer council). It explicitly states: "If 2 consecutive meetings feel low-value for either side, propose a quarterly cadence or a graceful pause." The baseline includes a tracking system but does not define relationship lifecycle management.

## Verdict

The skill output transforms mentor/sponsor-finding from a networking exercise into a relationship operating system. Its main advantage is the sponsor strategy -- the earn-signal-ask framework gives the PM a concrete path from "thin network" to "someone advocates for me in staffing decisions," which is the stated goal. The baseline is a well-organized practical guide that would serve someone new to intentional networking, but it does not close the gap between "having conversations" and "getting sponsored."
