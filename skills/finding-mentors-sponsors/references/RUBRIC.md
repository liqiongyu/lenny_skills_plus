# Rubric — Finding Mentors & Sponsors (Score 0/1/2)

0 = missing or unusable | 1 = present but weak | 2 = clear, specific, and reusable

Suggested passing bar: **>= 10/14**

## 1) Goal clarity (0–2)
- **0:** Goal is vague (“grow my career”) with no timeline, no specific gaps, and no success criteria.
- **1:** Clear outcomes exist but timeline is missing or context is thin; success criteria are qualitative only (“feel more confident”) with no observable behaviors.
- **2:** Specific, time-bounded outcomes (e.g., “get promotion-ready feedback from 3 senior PMs in 3 months”) with constraints, success criteria, and explicit assumptions.

## 2) Mentor vs sponsor differentiation (0–2)
- **0:** Treats mentors and sponsors as the same thing; plan does not distinguish between advice-giving and advocacy.
- **1:** Definitions exist and are stated, but the plan, outreach scripts, and asks do not change based on type — same approach for both.
- **2:** Clear distinctions with separate tactics: mentor asks focus on advice/feedback/coaching; sponsor asks focus on visibility, staffing, introductions, and advocacy in decision rooms. Deliverables differ by type.

## 3) Targeting quality (0–2)
- **0:** Random list of names with no fit rationale; fewer than 5 candidates; no diversification across pools.
- **1:** Some fit rationale exists but prioritization is weak; list is concentrated in one pool (e.g., only internal) or lacks profiles for top targets.
- **2:** 10-25 candidates across 3 pools (internal, adjacent network, external), each with a fit rationale linked to specific gaps; top 5 are high-fit with clear profiles and reachability assessment.

## 4) Warm-path realism (0–2)
- **0:** Mostly cold outreach with no angle, no mutual connections identified, and no community leverage.
- **1:** Some warm paths identified for a few targets, but fewer than 50% of top targets have a plausible warm path; first steps are vague.
- **2:** Strong warm paths for top targets (mutuals, shared communities, shared work); at least 50% of top 10 have a concrete first step; cold targets have a credible angle (shared interest, content, event).

## 5) Outreach quality (0–2)
- **0:** Generic messages (“Would you be my mentor?”), long, guilt-inducing, or missing entirely.
- **1:** Scripts exist but are not personalized per target, or they ask for open-ended commitments instead of small concrete asks (15-30 min); follow-up sequence is missing.
- **2:** Personalized, humble, specific, short scripts that name the gap, propose a small ask, offer an easy decline, and include a follow-up sequence and warm-intro request template.

## 6) Operating system quality (0–2)
- **0:** Plan ends after first conversations; no cadence, no tracking, no value exchange plan.
- **1:** Cadence exists (e.g., “monthly check-ins”) but tracking is informal and value exchange is not articulated — the relationship is one-directional.
- **2:** Sustainable cadence with a tracking table (next touchpoint, last interaction, value exchanged), clear value exchange plan (what you give back), and explicit rules for when to escalate or wind down a relationship.

## 7) Sponsorship activation (0–2)
- **0:** “Please sponsor me” with no earn-signal-ask plan; no concrete sponsor actions defined.
- **1:** Some actions exist (e.g., “increase visibility”) but asks are vague or poorly timed; no specific deliverables that demonstrate bet-worthiness.
- **2:** Clear earn/signal/ask path: specific “bet-worthy” outcomes to deliver first, visibility plan (pre-wires, demos, written updates), and concrete sponsor asks with timing (“introduce me to X before Y review cycle”).

## Interpreting the score
- **12–14:** Ready to execute immediately.
- **10–11:** Solid; tighten the weakest 1–2 areas.
- **< 10:** Rework targeting, outreach specificity, and/or sponsor activation plan.
