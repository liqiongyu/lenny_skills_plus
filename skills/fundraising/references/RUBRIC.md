# Fundraising — Scoring Rubric (Score 0/1/2)

0 = missing or unusable | 1 = present but weak | 2 = clear, specific, and reusable

Suggested passing bar: **>= 9/12**

## 1) Decision quality — raise vs don’t (0–2)
- **0:** No real decision analysis; assumes “raise money” is the right answer without examining alternatives or trade-offs.
- **1:** Trade-offs are listed (VC vs bootstrap, dilution, speed) but the recommendation is vague — no falsifiable criteria, no clear “raise if X by date Y” threshold.
- **2:** Clear decision criteria with a falsifiable recommendation (“raise if X metric by Y date; otherwise delay/bootstrap”); realistic alternatives (revenue-first, angels, strategic, debt) are evaluated honestly, not hand-waved.

## 2) Round design coherence (0–2)
- **0:** Amount, timeline, and milestone are disconnected or missing; use-of-funds is absent; investor profile is undefined.
- **1:** Some connection between amount and milestone, but assumptions are hidden or the investor profile is generic (“VCs who invest in SaaS”) without stage/check-size/thesis specifics.
- **2:** Amount range ties directly to a specific milestone with explicit runway math; use-of-funds narrative is clear; investor profile specifies stage, check size, thesis, and geography; all assumptions are labeled.

## 3) Narrative clarity + first impression (0–2)
- **0:** Generic story (“we’re disrupting X”); unclear ICP/outcome; no one-liner; first slide would not earn a second meeting.
- **1:** Clear story with problem/solution, but proof is weak (no metrics or proxies), the first slide is muddled, or the one-liner requires explanation.
- **2:** Crisp one-liner that passes the 10-second test; strong first slide (what it is, for whom, why it matters); evidence-backed narrative (metrics, traction, or validated proxies) that earns the next meeting.

## 4) Investor fit + targeting (0–2)
- **0:** Random investor list with no ICP, no fit rationale, and no warm-path analysis.
- **1:** Basic list with some rationale, but fewer than 50% of targets have fit reasons or warm paths; no sequencing strategy (who to approach first).
- **2:** ICP-driven list with fit reasons per investor (thesis match, portfolio adjacency, check size), warm paths identified for top targets, and a realistic sequencing strategy (friendlies first, tier-1 targets after pitch iteration).

## 5) Process executability (0–2)
- **0:** No pipeline tracker, no outreach cadence, no next steps; the user has no way to run the process after reading the pack.
- **1:** Some steps exist but are not schedulable — e.g., “reach out to investors” without weekly targets, follow-up timing, or a tracking template.
- **2:** Pipeline tracker with status fields; weekly activity targets (intros, meetings, follow-ups); follow-up SOP (timing, templates); next-14-days action plan with specific tasks and dates.

## 6) Risk handling + safety (0–2)
- **0:** Ignores legal/financial constraints; overshares confidential data; offers legal/tax opinions; no risks or open questions section.
- **1:** Mentions constraints and includes a risks section, but doesn’t operationalize them — e.g., no “questions for counsel” list, no guidance on what not to share with investors.
- **2:** Clear boundaries with a “questions for counsel” list; sensitive info guidance (what to share vs. hold back); explicit risks and open questions with mitigation strategies; no legal/tax/securities opinions.

## Interpreting the score
- **11–12:** Ready to execute immediately.
- **9–10:** Solid; tighten the weakest area.
- **< 9:** Rework decision quality, narrative, or process executability before shipping.
