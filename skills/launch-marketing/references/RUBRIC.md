# Rubric (Score 0–2)

Score the pack before finalizing. A “ship-ready” pack typically scores **≥ 10/14** with no dimension at 0.

## 1) Clarity of message (0–2)
- **0:** Core message is missing or jargon-heavy. No single CTA identified. A teammate could not repeat the message after one read.
- **1:** Message exists and is understandable, but competes with multiple CTAs or angles. The “one sentence” test passes only with effort.
- **2:** One clear message + one CTA that a teammate can repeat after a single read. The message is plain-language, specific, and avoids buzzwords.

## 2) Strength of hook/sizzle (0–2)
- **0:** No hook identified, or hook is pure hype with nothing showable (e.g., “AI-powered,” “revolutionary”). No demo, data, or visual artifact backs it.
- **1:** Hook exists and has some concreteness (a screenshot, a stat, a before/after), but differentiation from competitors is weak or the proof is thin.
- **2:** Hook is memorable, concrete, and supported by a showable demo, artifact, or stat. It earns attention independently of the full product story and is clearly different from competitor launches.

## 3) Audience/channel fit (0–2)
- **0:** Channels chosen without audience analysis. No sequencing or timing. No metrics per channel.
- **1:** Channels map to where the audience actually is. Basic sequencing exists, but some channels lack a specific message angle, owner, or success metric.
- **2:** Channel plan has a clear sequence (e.g., exclusive PR -> owned -> community -> partners), each channel has a named owner, a tailored message angle, a single CTA, and a success metric. Asset checklist is complete.

## 4) PR readiness (if applicable) (0–2)
- **0:** No pitch draft, or pitch is long, self-centered, and has unsubstantiated claims. No exclusive/broad strategy.
- **1:** Pitch templates exist and are reporter-centric. Exclusive vs. broad decision is made, but sequencing, fallback plan, or follow-up cadence has gaps.
- **2:** Pitch is concise, reporter-centric, with strong subject lines and proof bullets. Exclusive sequencing has clear timelines, decision points, and a defined fallback path. Follow-up cadence is respectful and scheduled.

## 5) Internal readiness quality (0–2)
- **0:** No talk track, FAQ, or objection handling. Sales/support teams would improvise on launch day.
- **1:** Talk track and FAQ exist, but objection handling is incomplete or escalation paths are missing. A support rep could answer “what is this” but not “why should I care” or “what about X competitor.”
- **2:** Talk track (30-second + detailed), FAQ, objection responses, and escalation path are complete. A sales/support teammate could handle the top 5 launch-day questions without guessing. Day-of runbook has timeline and monitoring plan.

## 6) Measurement + iteration (0–2)
- **0:** No metrics defined. No experiment plan. No instrumentation assumptions.
- **1:** Success metrics are listed and instrumentation assumptions are noted, but experiments lack thresholds or owners. No “double down” or “cut” rules.
- **2:** Metrics + instrumentation assumptions + experiment backlog with thresholds and owners. Explicit “double down” rule (what signal triggers scaling a channel) and “cut” rule (what signal triggers stopping). Post-launch review cadence is scheduled.

## 7) Safety and truthfulness (0–2)
- **0:** Claims are fabricated or unsubstantiated. No approval checkpoints. Confidential information is exposed.
- **1:** Claims are mostly safe, but some statements lack proof or approval flags. Legal/compliance review is mentioned but not built into the timeline.
- **2:** Every claim is substantiated or explicitly labeled as a placeholder. Approval checkpoints are built into the timeline. Legal/compliance flags are explicit. No confidential information is shared without authorization.
