# Rubric (0–2)

Score the Media Relations Pack. A “ship-ready” pack typically scores **≥ 9/12** with no dimension at 0.

## 1) Newsworthiness + proof (0–2)
- **0:** No “what’s new” or “why now” is articulated. Claims are vague hype (e.g., “revolutionary platform”). No proof points or evidence cited.
- **1:** An angle exists with a stated “what’s new” and “why now,” but proof is partial. Some claims lack evidence or placeholders are not flagged for follow-up.
- **2:** Angle is crisp with a clear “new information” claim, a specific “why now” reason, and at least one substantiated proof point per angle. Unknowns are explicitly labeled with “to validate” and a next step.

## 2) Targeting + personalization (0–2)
- **0:** Media list is a generic dump of outlet names with no beat alignment, no hooks, and no rationale for inclusion.
- **1:** Media list is tiered (Tier 1/2/3) and targets are mapped to beats, but fewer than half have a personalized hook referencing recent coverage or reporter interests.
- **2:** Every target on the tiered list has a beat-aligned rationale, a personalized hook referencing the reporter’s recent work or stated interests, and a specific angle tailored to that outlet’s audience.

## 3) Exclusive/embargo strategy (0–2)
- **0:** No exclusive/embargo strategy, or the plan has unrealistic timelines that ignore reporter lead times and editorial cycles.
- **1:** Exclusive or embargo decision is made with a basic timeline, but decision points are vague. Fallback plan exists but lacks specifics (e.g., “pitch others if declined” without a named backup list or timeline).
- **2:** Staggered plan has explicit timelines aligned to reporter cycles, clear decision points (e.g., “if no response by Day X, move to backup list”), a named fallback list, and explicit terms for what the exclusive/embargo includes.

## 4) Pitch kit quality (0–2)
- **0:** Pitch is long (>300 words), self-centered, or reads like a press release. No subject line options. Claims are unsubstantiated.
- **1:** Pitch templates are under 200 words and reporter-centric. Subject lines exist. Most claims have proof, but follow-up emails or CTAs need refinement.
- **2:** Pitch is concise (<150 words body), leads with the reporter’s interest, includes 2-3 subject line options, has proof bullets for every claim, a clear CTA, and a follow-up schedule. Templates cover exclusive, standard, and follow-up variants.

## 5) Execution readiness (tracker + cadence + materials) (0–2)
- **0:** No outreach tracker. No follow-up cadence. Press materials checklist is missing or incomplete.
- **1:** Outreach tracker exists with status columns and next actions. Follow-up cadence is defined. Press materials checklist exists but has gaps (e.g., missing bio, media FAQ, or asset links).
- **2:** Outreach tracker has columns for target, status, dates, notes, and next action. Follow-up cadence has specific day-counts and templates. Press materials checklist is complete (press release/blog, media FAQ, spokesperson bio, assets/screenshots, key data points). All materials are ready or have named owners and deadlines.

## 6) Risk management + integrity (0–2)
- **0:** Fabricated or unsubstantiated claims. No approval workflow. Sensitive information is exposed without guardrails.
- **1:** Claims are mostly substantiated. Approval checkpoints are mentioned but not integrated into the timeline. Some sensitive topics lack safe responses.
- **2:** Every claim is substantiated or explicitly labeled as a placeholder. Approval checkpoints are built into the outreach timeline. Sensitive topics have pre-approved safe responses. A corrections plan exists for post-publication errors. No confidential information is shared without explicit authorization.
