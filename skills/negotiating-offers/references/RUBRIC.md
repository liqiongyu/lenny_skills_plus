# Rubric (Score 0/1/2)

0 = missing or unusable | 1 = present but weak | 2 = clear, executable, reusable

Suggested passing bar: **>= 12/16**

## 1) Boundaries + safety (0–2)
- **0:** No boundaries stated; gives risky advice (e.g., encourages bluffing, asks for confidential employer data, or offers legal/tax opinions).
- **1:** Mentions boundaries (disclaimers present) but does not operationalize them — e.g., says "consult a lawyer" without specifying which items need legal review.
- **2:** Clear boundaries integrated into the workflow; legal/tax items are flagged with specific "questions for counsel"; no requests for secrets; safe defaults for sensitive topics (e.g., redacted numbers, honest BATNA framing).

## 2) Inputs captured (0–2)
- **0:** Missing core elements — no offer details, no timeline, no priorities; the reader cannot reconstruct the negotiation context.
- **1:** Partial intake — offer stage and some details present, but assumptions are unlabeled or more than 5 questions were asked at once.
- **2:** Clear snapshot with offer state (verbal vs written), timeline, key stakeholders, and priorities; assumptions are explicitly labeled; intake used 3-5 questions max per round.

## 3) Decision clarity (0–2)
- **0:** No BATNA defined; no deadline captured; priorities are unclear or contradictory (e.g., "must-have" on both comp and flexibility without acknowledging trade-off).
- **1:** Some clarity — BATNA or walk-away line exists but is vague ("I have options"); priority list has internal conflicts that are not resolved.
- **2:** Must/trade/nice/no-go categories are consistent and usable; BATNA is stated in plain language with a concrete walk-away threshold; deadline is captured with extension strategy if needed.

## 4) Success conditions quality (0–2)
- **0:** No resource or role-success asks; negotiation is purely about compensation with no attention to whether the user can succeed in the role.
- **1:** Asks exist but are vague ("I need support") or not tied to specific business outcomes; no verification method proposed.
- **2:** Outcome-tied asks (headcount, budget, authority, scope) with specific verification signals (written role charter, org commitment, OKR alignment) and a hiring-manager agenda to discuss them.

## 5) Tradeoff matrix quality (0–2)
- **0:** Flat list of asks with no prioritization, no explicit trades, and no rationale for what matters most.
- **1:** Some tradeoffs are mentioned but the "give-to-get" logic is unclear; features vs experiences distinction is not applied.
- **2:** Clear levers (comp and non-comp), explicit priorities with rationale, specific trades ("I’ll accept X if I get Y"), and features-vs-experiences framing where relevant.

## 6) Ask packaging + strategy (0–2)
- **0:** No A/B/C packages; no sequencing; asks are presented as a flat wish list.
- **1:** Packages exist but are internally inconsistent (e.g., C floor is not genuinely acceptable) or the sequence ignores timeline/leverage constraints.
- **2:** Coherent A/B/C packages where each is internally consistent; C is genuinely acceptable; sequencing matches constraints, leverage, and stakeholder dynamics (e.g., resources before comp).

## 7) Scripts quality (0–2)
- **0:** No scripts; only generic advice like "be confident" or "ask for more."
- **1:** Scripts exist but are too long, vague, or not role-appropriate; missing follow-up recap or key conversation branches.
- **2:** Sendable, role-appropriate scripts (email + call agenda + follow-up recap); include polite if/then branches; claims are truthful; no threats or bluffs.

## 8) Finish quality (0–2)
- **0:** Missing risks, open questions, or next steps entirely.
- **1:** Risks/open questions/next steps are included but generic ("things could go wrong") without specifics.
- **2:** Specific risks with mitigation strategies; open questions with a plan to resolve them; next actions have dates, owners, and specific deliverables (e.g., "send recruiter email by Tuesday").
