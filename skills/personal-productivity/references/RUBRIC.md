# Rubric — Personal Productivity System Pack (Score 0/1/2)

0 = missing or unusable | 1 = present but weak | 2 = clear, specific, and reusable

Suggested passing bar: **>= 9/12**

## 1) Fit to constraints (0–2)
- **0:** Ignores hard constraints (timezone, caregiving, on-call, travel); the default week is unrealistic and would require the user to change their life, not their system.
- **1:** Mostly fits constraints, but 1-2 conflicts remain unresolved (e.g., a focus block during a mandatory meeting window); trade-offs are implicit.
- **2:** Clearly respects all stated constraints; trade-offs between competing demands are explicit and resolved; side commitments (advising, board) are accounted for.

## 2) Timebox plan quality (0–2)
- **0:** Vague intentions (“do deep work in the morning”) with no actual blocks, no meeting strategy, and no buffer time.
- **1:** Has time blocks on a calendar, but rules are weak — e.g., focus blocks have no booking protection, buffers are aspirational, or meeting windows are undefined.
- **2:** Clear meeting windows with booking rules, 3+ focus blocks/week with protection mechanism, explicit buffer time between high-load blocks, and a weekend-spillover rule.

## 3) Capture/to-do system simplicity (0–2)
- **0:** Over-engineered (5+ lists, multiple tools, unclear routing) or missing entirely; a new task has no clear “home.”
- **1:** System works but has friction — e.g., too many lists, processing takes >15 minutes/day, or the prioritization rule is vague (“do important things first”).
- **2:** One inbox, small taxonomy (3-5 lists max), a daily processing ritual under 15 minutes, and a clear prioritization rule (e.g., timebox top 3 on calendar, defer rest).

## 4) Rituals practicality (0–2)
- **0:** Rituals are long (30+ minutes each), aspirational, or missing; no shutdown routine; no weekly review.
- **1:** Rituals are defined but may be hard to sustain — e.g., morning routine requires 45 minutes, or weekly review has no checklist and would drift.
- **2:** Morning “top outcomes” takes under 10 minutes; shutdown ritual is a copy/paste checklist under 10 minutes; weekly review is under 30 minutes with a structured checklist; total ritual overhead is under 60 minutes/day.

## 5) Testability + rollout (0–2)
- **0:** No measurement criteria, no rollout plan; user is expected to adopt everything at once.
- **1:** Some measures exist (e.g., “fewer dropped tasks”) but the rollout is generic (“start next week”) with no staging or decision rules for what to keep/drop.
- **2:** 7-day rollout plan with staged changes (day 1-2 setup, day 3-4 first ritual, day 5-7 iterate); 2-3 measurable signals (e.g., dropped tasks/week, deep-work blocks completed); decision rules for week 2 adjustments.

## 6) Clarity + actionability (0–2)
- **0:** Generic advice (“be more organized”) with no concrete next actions, no artifacts, and no rules the user can follow.
- **1:** Concrete in places but missing specifics — e.g., “use a to-do app” without specifying the processing rule, or “do a weekly review” without a checklist.
- **2:** Clear artifacts (timebox plan, system spec, ritual checklists), explicit rules, and specific next steps; a new user could follow the pack without additional guidance.

## Interpreting the score
- **11–12:** Ready to execute immediately.
- **9–10:** Solid; tighten the weakest area.
- **< 9:** Rework timebox plan, system simplicity, or rollout before shipping.
