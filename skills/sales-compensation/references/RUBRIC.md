# Sales Compensation — Rubric (Score 0–2)

Score each dimension from **0 (absent)** to **2 (strong)**. A "ship-ready" pack scores **≥ 10/14** with no dimension at **0**.

## 1) Clarity + simplicity
**0 — Absent:** Metric definitions are ambiguous or undefined; the plan has numerous special cases and exceptions that make it impossible to explain in one page.
**1 — Partial:** Mostly clear definitions, but some terms require interpretation (e.g., "qualified deal" undefined) or there are too many exceptions (3+) that create confusion.
**2 — Strong:** The plan can be explained on one page. All metrics have unambiguous definitions. Exceptions are minimal (0-2) and clearly documented. A rep can estimate their payout mentally after closing a deal.

## 2) Role controllability
**0 — Absent:** Reps are paid or penalized on outcomes they cannot influence (e.g., SDRs paid on closed-won revenue, AEs penalized for implementation churn they do not control).
**1 — Partial:** Primary metric is reasonably controllable, but secondary metrics or adjustments include factors outside the rep's sphere (e.g., NRR multiplier where rep has no post-sale influence).
**2 — Strong:** Every metric maps directly to role responsibilities. If retention alignment is included, the measurement window and rep influence are explicitly justified (e.g., 90-day clawback only for deals where rep set expectations).

## 3) Incentive alignment (anti-gaming)
**0 — Absent:** Plan strongly incentivizes harmful behaviors — deep discounting, pipeline stuffing, closing bad-fit deals, or pulling revenue forward with no guardrails.
**1 — Partial:** Some guardrails exist (e.g., discount approval threshold) but obvious loopholes remain (e.g., no minimum deal size, no churn consequence, no multi-year discount cap).
**2 — Strong:** Common gaming behaviors are identified and neutralized. Discount guardrails, minimum deal thresholds, and retention alignment are in place. Accelerators reward the right behavior (e.g., multi-year, strategic segment) without creating perverse incentives.

## 4) Economic viability
**0 — Absent:** Plan economics are not modeled. At high attainment, costs could spiral unpredictably. No scenario analysis.
**1 — Partial:** Basic economics are viable under the base case, but accelerators, draws, or guarantees are not stress-tested under upside or downside scenarios.
**2 — Strong:** Plan is modeled under low/base/high scenarios. Total comp cost as a percentage of revenue is bounded. Accelerators are intentional and affordable at 150%+ attainment. CAC payback and margin impact are considered.

## 5) Ramp realism
**0 — Absent:** New reps are on full quota from day one with no draw, guarantee, or reduced targets. Ramp time is not acknowledged.
**1 — Partial:** A ramp schedule exists (e.g., reduced quota months 1-3) but is not tied to actual productivity drivers (pipeline build time, training completion, first-deal timeline) or does not include a draw/guarantee.
**2 — Strong:** Ramp schedule reflects realistic time-to-productivity with clear milestones. Draw or guarantee covers the ramp period. Ramp targets are activity-based early (pipeline created, demos run) and outcome-based later (bookings).

## 6) Retention/quality alignment
**0 — Absent:** Plan pays purely on bookings with no mechanism to account for churn, customer fit, or long-term value. Reps are rewarded identically for a churning customer and a retained one.
**1 — Partial:** Retention is mentioned (e.g., "we'll add a clawback") but the mechanism is vague — no measurement window, no clear trigger, or not integrated into the plan math.
**2 — Strong:** A specific retention-alignment method is defined (holdback, clawback, NRR multiplier, or collected-revenue basis) with clear measurement window, trigger conditions, and dispute resolution. The mechanism is fair and proportional to rep influence.

## 7) Operability + governance
**0 — Absent:** Payouts require manual spreadsheet work. No CRM field definitions. No dispute or exception process. No change control.
**1 — Partial:** Admin process exists but is brittle — e.g., payout calculation depends on fields not consistently tracked, or dispute process is informal ("talk to your manager").
**2 — Strong:** Required CRM/billing fields are defined. Payout calculation process is documented and auditable. Dispute resolution has a clear escalation path. Change control policy specifies notice period and grandfathering rules.

## Decision rule
- **Ship** if total score is **≥ 10/14** and no dimension scores **0**.
- **Revise** if any dimension scores **0** or total is below **10**.
