# Rubric (Score 0–2)

Score each dimension 0/1/2:
- 0 = missing / unusable
- 1 = present but weak
- 2 = clear, executable, and likely to produce alignment

Suggested passing bar: **>= 10/14**.

## 1) Alignment target clarity (0–2)
- **0:** “Alignment” is vague; no named decision, commitment, or timeline. A reader cannot finish the sentence “After alignment, stakeholders will commit to _____.”
- **1:** A decision/commitment exists but scope or timeline is fuzzy, or the goal conflates inform/align/decide without distinguishing them.
- **2:** Decision/commitment, owner(s), deadline, and “done” definition are explicit and stable. The alignment target could be read aloud to any stakeholder without confusion.

## 2) Stakeholder coverage + decision rights (0–2)
- **0:** Missing key functions (e.g., Legal, Finance, Eng); decision rights unclear; high risk of hidden veto or “silent blocker.”
- **1:** Most stakeholders covered, but influence/roles are ambiguous or the no-surprises list is incomplete.
- **2:** Decider, approvers, influencers, implementers, and blockers are mapped with clear roles. Every critical function is included or explicitly scoped out.

## 3) “How they think” accuracy (0–2)
- **0:** Stakeholders treated as a monolith; individual incentives, evidence preferences, and objection patterns are ignored.
- **1:** Some tailoring exists (e.g., per-person notes), but decision principles and evidence preferences are generic or incomplete.
- **2:** Clear decision principles per key stakeholder with predicted objections and tailored evidence. For each key person, you can explain “in what world does their viewpoint make sense.”

## 4) User value + constraint framing (0–2)
- **0:** Proposal reads as “nice to have”; constraints and tradeoffs are missing. A skeptical stakeholder could dismiss it without engaging.
- **1:** Some framing exists, but the vital question, why-now, or business constraints are not crisp enough to anchor discussion.
- **2:** Simple, compelling user-value anchor plus explicit business constraints and tradeoffs. The proposal cannot be dismissed without disputing a stated assumption.

## 5) Criteria quality (0–2)
- **0:** Evaluation criteria are missing, or there are too many (>10) with no real tradeoffs.
- **1:** Criteria exist but do not map to stakeholder principles, or they are so generic that any option could pass.
- **2:** 3-7 meaningful criteria that map to stakeholder principles and force genuine tradeoffs. Two reviewers would score options the same way.

## 6) Pre-brief effectiveness (0–2)
- **0:** No pre-briefs planned; high risk of live-meeting blowup with surprised stakeholders.
- **1:** Some pre-briefs planned but not targeted to the decider/blockers, or objections are not captured in a change log.
- **2:** Targeted pre-brief loop covers all key stakeholders; objections are captured; the pack is updated with a change log; no major stakeholder sees the core ask for the first time in the live meeting.

## 7) Commitment capture + follow-through (0–2)
- **0:** Meeting ends with “general agreement” or “good discussion” but no named owners, dates, or written decisions.
- **1:** Some owners and decisions exist, but follow-up comms or review checkpoint is missing or vague.
- **2:** Decisions, owners, dates, rationale, and a review/revisit checkpoint are captured in writing and communicated to all stakeholders (including those not in the room).

## Interpreting scores
- **12–14:** Strong. Proceed with stakeholders.
- **9–11:** Medium risk. Fix the lowest 2 categories before the live meeting.
- **< 9:** High risk. Rebuild the alignment target, stakeholder map, and criteria.
