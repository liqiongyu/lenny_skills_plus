# Scoring rubric (0–2 per criterion)

Use this rubric to score shortlisted ideas. Adapt weights to your constraints.

Scale:
- **0** = weak / missing
- **1** = plausible but unproven
- **2** = strong with evidence or clear advantage

## Criteria definitions

### 1) Off-the-beaten-path insight
- 0: Generic idea; no unique signal or lived experience
- 1: Some signal (anecdote) but not clearly differentiated
- 2: Strong unique insight/access (repeated workflow exposure, niche community, proprietary access)

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** The idea could have been generated by anyone reading TechCrunch; no founder-specific signal, lived experience, or privileged access is cited.
- **1:** A personal anecdote or observation is mentioned, but it is a single data point without pattern confirmation; others in the space likely see the same thing.
- **2:** The founder cites repeated exposure (e.g., "managed 200+ vendor onboardings"), names a specific niche community or data source, or has proprietary access that competitors lack.

### 2) Why Now strength
- 0: No Why Now; could be done anytime
- 1: Some shift mentioned, but vague or not clearly enabling
- 2: Clear enabling shift with a convincing "why not earlier" explanation

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No temporal rationale; the idea could have been pursued 5 years ago with the same tools and market conditions.
- **1:** A trend is named ("AI is improving", "remote work") but the connection to this specific idea is hand-wavy; no "why not earlier" is articulated.
- **2:** A concrete shift is identified (new API capability, regulatory change, cost curve crossing a threshold) with a clear explanation of what was impossible or uneconomical before this shift.

### 3) Pain severity + urgency
- 0: Nice-to-have; low stakes; unclear budget owner
- 1: Real pain but sporadic or weak willingness-to-pay signals
- 2: High-stakes pain (time/money/risk) with strong urgency/willingness-to-pay indicators

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** The problem is a minor inconvenience; no one loses money, time, or compliance standing if it stays unsolved; no identifiable budget holder.
- **1:** Users acknowledge the pain when asked but are not actively seeking solutions; willingness-to-pay is hypothetical rather than demonstrated.
- **2:** Users are spending significant time/money on workarounds (spreadsheets, manual processes, existing tools); a budget owner is identifiable and the pain recurs on a predictable cadence.

### 4) Wedge clarity (why you win)
- 0: "We'll build it better" / no wedge
- 1: Some differentiation, but easy for others to copy
- 2: Defensible wedge (workflow depth, data, distribution, regulatory, speed + insight)

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** Differentiation is "better UX" or "we will use AI"; no structural reason why this team wins over well-funded incumbents.
- **1:** A plausible angle exists (e.g., "we know the workflow") but it is easily replicable by any team that spends a month in the domain; no compounding advantage.
- **2:** The wedge names a specific structural advantage -- proprietary data loop, exclusive distribution channel, regulatory moat, or deep workflow integration -- that grows stronger with traction.

### 5) Distribution/GTM advantage
- 0: "We'll figure it out"; unclear channel
- 1: Plausible channel but no advantage
- 2: Credible channel advantage (existing network, partnerships, community access, clear sales motion)

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No go-to-market hypothesis; distribution is "we will do marketing" or "word of mouth" with no specifics.
- **1:** A channel is named (e.g., "LinkedIn outreach", "content marketing") but the team has no existing presence, network, or partnership in that channel; they start from zero.
- **2:** The team has a concrete distribution edge -- an existing audience, warm intros to 50+ target buyers, a partnership pipeline, or a community they already lead -- that gives them unfair early traction.

### 6) Tarpit risk (inverse)
- 0: High tarpit risk; depends on miracles (cold start, brutal CAC, winner-take-most) with no wedge
- 1: Some structural risk, but mitigations are plausible
- 2: Low structural risk, or a clear wedge that avoids the tarpit

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** The idea has a known structural trap (marketplace cold-start, consumer social network effects, hardware dependency, regulatory approval gate) and no credible plan to overcome it.
- **1:** A structural risk exists but a specific mitigation is proposed (e.g., "start single-player mode to avoid cold start"); mitigation is plausible but unproven.
- **2:** The idea avoids major structural traps by design (e.g., single-player value, supply-side aggregation, direct sales to known buyers), or the team has a demonstrated wedge that neutralizes the tarpit.

### 7) Feasibility (MVP in weeks)
- 0: Requires heavy R&D/integration/compliance; long path to MVP
- 1: MVP plausible but requires meaningful effort/unknowns
- 2: MVP can be built quickly with the team's current skills/tools

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** MVP requires 3+ months of R&D, regulatory approval, hardware procurement, or deep integrations the team has no experience with.
- **1:** MVP is buildable in 4-8 weeks but requires learning a new domain, building non-trivial integrations, or resolving meaningful technical unknowns.
- **2:** The team can ship a testable MVP (landing page + concierge, prototype, or working tool) within 2-3 weeks using their current stack and skills.

### 8) Fast validation path
- 0: Hard to test without building the full product
- 1: Can test with some effort, but signals may be noisy
- 2: Can test quickly with clear pass/fail signals (interviews, concierge, pilot, landing page, etc.)

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** The only way to test the idea is to build the full product and launch it; no intermediate signal is available (e.g., "we need 10K users to see if the algorithm works").
- **1:** A test is possible (e.g., interviews, fake door test) but the signal is indirect or noisy; pass/fail criteria are ambiguous.
- **2:** A specific test is designable in under 2 weeks with clear pass/fail criteria -- e.g., "10 interviews with target ICP; 7/10 describe this pain unprompted" or "landing page converts >5% of targeted traffic."

## Suggested interpretation
- Prefer ideas where the **top 2–3 criteria** align with your constraints (e.g., distribution + feasibility).
- If an idea wins only because of speculative scoring, downgrade and validate before committing.
