# Rubric (Startup Pivoting)

Score each item 0/1/2:
- 0 = missing / unusable
- 1 = present but incomplete
- 2 = clear, executable, reusable

Suggested passing bar: **>= 16/20**.

## 1) Decision framing (0-2)
- Owner + date + binary decision are explicit.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No decision owner named; no deadline; the framing is "let's explore options" with no commitment to decide.
- **1:** A decision owner or date is stated but not both; or the decision is framed ambiguously (e.g., "we should think about pivoting" rather than "pivot vs. persevere by [date]").
- **2:** Decision owner, decision date, and a binary/explicit decision statement are all present (e.g., "CEO decides pivot vs. persevere by March 15").

## 2) Evidence quality (0-2)
- Evidence is inventoried; gaps are labeled; assumptions are explicit.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No evidence cited; claims are opinion-based; no distinction between facts, assumptions, and gaps.
- **1:** Some evidence is cited (e.g., retention numbers, churn rate) but gaps are not labeled and assumptions are mixed in with facts without flagging.
- **2:** Evidence is inventoried in a structured format; each claim is tagged as fact/assumption/gap; the top 3 evidence gaps are named with a plan to fill them.

## 3) Diagnosis clarity (0-2)
- "What's stuck" is specific (not generic); bottlenecks are ranked.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** Diagnosis is "growth is slow" or "retention is bad" with no specificity about where in the funnel, which segment, or what changed.
- **1:** The stuck point is identified (e.g., "D30 retention is 8%") but root causes are not hypothesized or ranked; signal vs. execution issues are not separated.
- **2:** The top 1-3 bottlenecks are named and ranked; each has a hypothesis about root cause; the diagnosis clearly separates signal problems (wrong market/product) from execution problems (pricing/onboarding/distribution).

## 4) Exhaustion check integrity (0-2)
- Non-pivot levers are evaluated fairly and time-boxed where appropriate.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No exhaustion check; the analysis jumps directly to pivot options without considering whether the current direction was tested fairly.
- **1:** Some non-pivot levers are mentioned but evaluation is superficial (e.g., "we tried marketing" without specifics); no time-boxed "last best tries" are proposed.
- **2:** Each major non-pivot lever is listed with what was tried, how well it was executed, and whether it was given a fair test; remaining untried levers are either proposed as time-boxed experiments or explicitly ruled out with rationale.

## 5) Option set quality (0-2)
- 4-8 distinct options; includes at least one 200% pivot; each has "what must be true".

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** Fewer than 3 options; all are minor variations of the same idea; no 200% pivot; no "what must be true" statements.
- **1:** 3-4 options exist but they are mostly 10% tweaks; a 200% option may be mentioned but lacks a "what must be true" hypothesis; options are not clearly distinct from each other.
- **2:** 4-8 distinct options span the 4P grid (Problem, Persona, Product, Positioning); at least one is a genuine 200% pivot; each option has a clear "what must be true" statement and a plausible distribution path.

## 6) Thesis quality (0-2)
- Pivot thesis is falsifiable and internally consistent (persona/problem/promise/distribution).

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No pivot thesis articulated; or thesis is vague ("we will target enterprise") with no persona/problem/promise specificity.
- **1:** A thesis is stated but is not falsifiable (no way to disprove it in a time-box) or has internal inconsistencies (e.g., targeting SMBs with an enterprise sales motion).
- **2:** The thesis clearly names persona, problem, promise, and distribution; it is falsifiable within the stated time-box; internal elements are consistent and mutually reinforcing.

## 7) Metrics and gates (0-2)
- North Star + leading indicators + guardrails + kill criteria are concrete and dated.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No metrics defined; or metrics are vague ("improve retention") with no targets, baselines, or dates.
- **1:** A North Star metric is named but leading indicators are missing or targets lack deadlines; kill criteria are absent or are "we will know it when we see it."
- **2:** North Star metric has a baseline and target; 2-5 leading indicators are defined with weekly/biweekly check-in cadence; kill criteria are concrete and dated (e.g., "if <X by [date], we stop and re-evaluate").

## 8) Validation plan strength (0-2)
- Contains a hard-truth test; learning goals map to decisions.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** No validation plan; or plan is "build the product and see what happens" with no structured learning.
- **1:** A validation plan exists but lacks a hard-truth test (a test that could definitively disprove the thesis); learning goals are stated but do not clearly map to pivot/persevere/kill decisions.
- **2:** The plan includes at least one hard-truth test designed to falsify the pivot thesis; each learning goal is explicitly linked to a decision ("if we learn X, we do Y; if not, we do Z").

## 9) Execution realism (0-2)
- Plan fits runway and team capacity; includes cut list and ownership.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** Plan ignores runway constraints; no cut list; no ownership assigned; the pivot sprint is longer than available runway.
- **1:** Plan acknowledges runway and assigns some ownership, but the scope exceeds team capacity; cut list is absent or vague ("we will reduce scope as needed").
- **2:** Plan explicitly fits within runway; each workstream has an owner; a concrete cut list names what the team will stop doing; resource conflicts are addressed.

## 10) Shareability + safety (0-2)
- Memo is readable and decision-ready; includes risks/open questions/next steps; human checkpoint included.

**Boundary guide:**
- **0:** Output is disorganized notes, not a shareable memo; risks/open questions are absent; no human checkpoint is mentioned.
- **1:** The memo is structured but assumes too much context (a board member or new team member could not follow it); risks or next steps are present but incomplete; human checkpoint is implied but not explicit.
- **2:** The memo is self-contained and readable by someone with no prior context; risks, open questions, and next steps are each present with owners; the human decision checkpoint (who decides, by when) is prominently stated.
