# Rubric (score 1–5; 3 = acceptable)

## 1) Outcome alignment + scope clarity
- **0 (missing):** No outcomes defined; rituals are listed without any stated purpose.
- **1:** Rituals are generic ("weekly sync", "monthly review") with unclear outcomes; could apply to any team.
- **2:** Some outcomes are stated but rituals don't map to them clearly; one or more rituals lack a stated purpose.
- **3:** Outcomes are defined; rituals generally map to them; but some rituals serve overlapping purposes or one outcome has no ritual supporting it.
- **4:** Outcomes are crisp and every ritual maps to at least one outcome; minor gap in measurability of one outcome.
- **5:** Outcomes are crisp, measurable-ish, and every ritual earns its slot. The mapping table shows no gaps and no redundancy.

## 2) Minimalism + time efficiency
- **0 (missing):** No time budget stated; total meeting load is not calculated.
- **1:** Adds meetings or ignores time budget; total sync time exceeds what the team can sustain (e.g., >8 hours/week for a 6-person team).
- **2:** Time budget exists but is aspirational; no existing rituals were killed or made async; net meeting load increases.
- **3:** Fits within a stated time budget with some trade-offs; at least one status-only meeting is replaced with async.
- **4:** Actively manages time budget; most status is async; one or two sync rituals could still be shortened or merged.
- **5:** Actively reduces meeting load and replaces status with async patterns. Every sync minute is justified by a need for real-time interaction (decisions, alignment, learning).

## 3) Template + artifact quality (repeatability)
- **0 (missing):** No templates or artifact definitions for any ritual.
- **1:** No templates; outputs are vague (e.g., "discuss priorities" with no defined output artifact).
- **2:** Some templates exist but are incomplete or not consistently structured; some rituals produce no artifact.
- **3:** Templates exist and are usable; outputs are mostly clear; but some templates lack timeboxes or follow-up action tracking.
- **4:** Templates are structured with timeboxes and clear artifact outputs; minor gap (e.g., one template lacks a follow-up mechanism).
- **5:** Templates are timeboxed, artifact-first, and produce durable outputs (decisions/commitments/learnings). A team member who missed the meeting can reconstruct what happened from the artifact alone.

## 4) Adoption + onboarding readiness (first Friday)
- **0 (missing):** No onboarding materials for rituals.
- **1:** No onboarding; rituals rely on tribal knowledge; a new hire would not know what meetings exist or why.
- **2:** A list of rituals exists but lacks context (purpose, expectations, where to find templates); new hire must ask around.
- **3:** Onboarding primer exists; templates are findable; but a new hire could not run a ritual independently by first Friday.
- **4:** Onboarding primer explains purpose, norms, and templates for each ritual; a new hire could participate effectively by first Friday but may not yet be able to run one solo.
- **5:** A new hire can find, understand, and run a ritual by first Friday using clear docs and examples. The primer is a self-contained 1-pager.

## 5) Governance + iteration (avoid sprawl)
- **0 (missing):** No governance, ownership, or review mechanism.
- **1:** No owner or review cadence; rituals accumulate without any mechanism to retire them.
- **2:** Owners are assigned but there is no review cadence or retirement rules; rituals are assumed permanent.
- **3:** Owners and a review cadence exist (e.g., quarterly); but keep/change/kill criteria are not defined.
- **4:** Clear review cadence with keep/change/kill criteria; feedback loop exists; minor gap in retirement rules or escalation path when a ritual owner leaves.
- **5:** Clear keep/change/kill mechanism with explicit criteria, feedback loop, and retirement rules. Ritual count is actively managed to prevent sprawl.

## 6) Trust, inclusion, and async-friendliness
- **0 (missing):** No consideration of inclusion, async participation, or psychological safety.
- **1:** Rituals feel like surveillance or status-checking; exclude time zones or quiet voices; no async path.
- **2:** Some awareness of time zones or async needs but no concrete mechanisms; quiet voices are not explicitly accommodated.
- **3:** Basic async participation (e.g., written pre-reads) and psychological safety considerations exist; rotating times are mentioned but not operationalized.
- **4:** Rituals have async paths and rotating times for distributed teams; doc-first approach exists; minor gap in explicitly accommodating quiet voices or introverted styles.
- **5:** Rituals are inclusive by design (doc-first, async paths, rotating times, structured turn-taking) and improve trust. Every ritual has an async participation option for distributed team members.

## Scoring interpretation
- **24–30:** Excellent; ready to adopt.
- **18–23:** Good; address minor gaps.
- **12–17:** Risky; revise before rollout.
- **<12:** Not usable; redesign the system.
