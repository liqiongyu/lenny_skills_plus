# Rubric (1–5)

Score the Job Description Pack. A strong pack is typically **≥ 22/30** with no category below 3.

## 1) Outcome clarity (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No outcomes section exists; the JD is purely a list of tasks or traits.
- **1:** No 12-month outcomes; mostly tasks and traits (e.g., “manage projects”, “collaborate cross-functionally”).
- **2:** Outcomes are attempted but are actually activities in disguise (e.g., “own the roadmap” without specifying what success looks like).
- **3:** Outcomes exist but are vague or not tied to business impact (e.g., “improve the product” without metrics or owned artifacts).
- **4:** Outcomes are specific and tied to business impact; most have measurable indicators but 1-2 are still vague.
- **5:** Outcomes are specific, measurable where possible, and clearly tied to business impact + owned artifacts. A candidate can envision what “champagne-worthy” 12-month success looks like.

## 2) Spike definition (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No competency prioritization; skills are listed without hierarchy.
- **1:** Laundry list of 8+ skills; no prioritization; impossible to tell what matters most.
- **2:** Some grouping of skills but no clear major/minor distinction; “strong” is undefined.
- **3:** Some prioritization but unclear what “strong” looks like; spikes don’t map to specific outcomes.
- **4:** 1 major + 1-2 minors identified with definitions; minor gap in linking spikes to outcomes or evidence of strength.
- **5:** 1 major + 1-2 minors are crisp, evidence-backed (what to look for in portfolio/narratives), and directly linked to 12-month outcomes.

## 3) Signal & differentiation (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No company or team context provided.
- **1:** Generic JD could apply anywhere; no company-specific information beyond the name.
- **2:** Basic company description exists but role description is interchangeable with any similar role at any company.
- **3:** Some company context, but role still feels template-y; “why here” is generic (“fast-paced environment”, “great culture”).
- **4:** Clear “why here / why now” with some concrete constraints; missing one of: specific challenges, team context, or growth trajectory.
- **5:** Clear “why here / why now”, concrete constraints, and specific challenges that attract the right candidates. A candidate can tell this role apart from the same title at 5 other companies.

## 4) Honesty & filtering (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No truth section or filtering mechanism.
- **1:** Oversells or hides constraints; no filters; reads like marketing copy.
- **2:** Mentions some realities but hedges or buries them; “who will thrive” is generic (“self-starters”, “passionate”).
- **3:** Some truth statements but weak self-selection; a mismatched candidate might still apply without realizing it.
- **4:** Candid “what’s hard” section exists; “who will thrive / who won’t” is specific but could be sharper in one area.
- **5:** Candid, specific “what’s hard” + “who will thrive/won’t” that helps candidates self-select without bias. Constraints are stated factually, not as hype.

## 5) Inclusivity & compliance hygiene (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No consideration of inclusivity or compliance.
- **1:** Includes biased language (gendered terms, age proxies like “digital native”) or ambiguous requirements; missing basic guardrails.
- **2:** Some effort at inclusive language but still has jargon, unnecessary requirements (e.g., specific degree when not needed), or protected-class proxies.
- **3:** Mostly clean but still has jargon/bias risk or unclear constraints; EEO placeholder may be missing.
- **4:** Clear, inclusive language; most requirements are justified; minor gap (e.g., EEO placeholder present but accommodations not mentioned).
- **5:** Clear, inclusive language; constraints stated truthfully; EEO/accommodations placeholder included; avoids protected-class proxies. Must-haves vs nice-to-haves are clearly separated.

## 6) Iteration readiness (1–5)
- **0 (missing):** No mention of iteration or version tracking.
- **1:** Treated as a one-and-done document; no mechanism to learn from candidate signal.
- **2:** Vaguely mentions “we’ll update it” but no specific triggers, owners, or tracking.
- **3:** Mentions iteration but no triggers or tracking; owner may be unclear.
- **4:** Iteration triggers and owner defined; version log exists but is not yet tied to specific candidate signal (who’s applying, who’s dropping off).
- **5:** Has iteration triggers, owners, and a version log tied to candidate signal. Clear mechanism for learning from pipeline data.
